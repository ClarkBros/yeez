Yeez writes and publishes for you every day, and Learn fine-tunes your headlines as the numbers come in. Bigger moves (what kinds of stories you run, how many a day, how headlines are written) are yours to make. Your weekly review does the analysis and lays out the options. This is how to read it and make good calls. It's the same method the Yeez team uses on its own sites.

## 1. Start from one number

Pick a views-per-day goal for each site and a date. Everything else is judged against it. Type `/yeez:review` and say "set my views goal" (or change it any time). The review shows where you are, the gap, and what it takes to close it.

A good first goal is one your site has already hit: your best month's daily views. Once you're there, set the next one.

## 2. Only trust real numbers, over the full history

One good day or one bad week tells you almost nothing. The review compares each post with others published the same month on the same platform. "Lift" is that comparison: 1.0 is average for its month, 2.0 is twice as many views. Two more things to keep in mind:
- **The current month always looks low.** Its posts haven't had time to collect views.
- **Under about 15 posts is a hunch, not a pattern.** Ask the review how many posts a finding rests on.

## 3. Find what's actually moving your views

When views are flat or falling, the cause is almost always one of these. The review checks each one:
- **Story types.** Some kinds of stories pull several times your average and some pull a fraction of it. Check how much of your day goes to each. The most common leak is a lot of slots going to stories your readers skip.
- **Headline shape.** Length, whether it opens with a person, a number or a quote, emotion and curiosity words. What wins differs from site to site and platform to platform, so go by your own numbers, not general advice.
- **How articles open.** Winners usually open with the person and the moment. Losers often open like a press release.
- **Volume.** More posts only help while views per post hold. The review shows whether weeks with more posts kept their views per post.
- **Distribution.** If the platform didn't make your posts live, nothing else matters. Check feed boxes, flagged images and quality notices first.
- **Proven winners.** Big hits from months ago can often run again with today's facts and a fresh headline.

## 4. Question your own rules

Every rule you've saved controls part of your day. Ask of each one: what do the numbers say about it? A rule that keeps a story type your readers love to one a day, or pushes slots into stories that get a fraction of your average, quietly costs views every single day. Changing a rule that's costing you is usually the fastest win there is.

## 5. Do the math of the goal

To raise views per day you can publish more, get more views per post, or land more hits (big posts). The review works out each one for your site: the views per post you'd need, the extra hits a week, and whether your site has ever done that. If it has never been done, take a step goal first.

## 6. Change one thing at a time

Pick one change, run it 7 days (a whole-site change like the daily count or the mix) or 14 days (a headline or story-type test), then judge it on views:
- **Grow** it if it clearly beat the rest.
- **Hold** it another week if it's too close to call.
- **End** it if it lost. That's a result too: you've learned what not to do.

Change two things at once and you'll never know which one worked. The review keeps a log of every test and opens each week with how the running one did.

## 7. Ask it anything

The review is a conversation. Reply in the same chat, or type `/yeez:review`, and ask what you'd ask an analyst: "why did views drop in October?", "show me every post over 20,000 views this month", "do longer headlines do better on my garden site?", "what if I went to 15 a day?" It answers from your own numbers and shows the posts behind them. Got an idea of your own? Tell it. It'll check the idea against your data and set it up as your next test.
